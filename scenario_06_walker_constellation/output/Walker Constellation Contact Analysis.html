Fleet Summary — 24-hour window
Key Findings
Tromso blackout — constellation geometry ceiling
All 24 satellites returned 0 contacts with Tromso (69.7°N) across the full 24-hour window. At 51.6° inclination the sub-satellite track peaks at ±51.6° latitude — 18° short of Tromso. This is not a simulation artifact; it is a hard geometric ceiling of the Walker 24/3/1 at ISS inclination. No satellite in any plane ever rises above Tromso's horizon during this epoch.
London dominates — 5–6 passes per satellite
London (51.5°N) sits almost exactly at the inclination ceiling, giving it near-maximum dwell time. Every satellite produces 5–6 contacts totalling 1,900–2,600 s (~32–43 min cumulative coverage). The full 24-satellite fleet delivers 128 passes over London in a single day — roughly one visible satellite every 11 minutes on average.
Equatorial stations (Nairobi & Lagos) see 2–5 passes per satellite
Near-equatorial stations are overflown at shallower geometry and on fewer passes than London. Nairobi averages 3.3 passes/satellite (79 total); Lagos averages 3.4 passes/satellite (81 total). Both stations share similar totals — consistent with their near-equatorial latitudes — but individual satellites vary between 2 and 5 contacts depending on orbital phase at epoch.
Pass durations: 5–9 min per event, clipped passes at interval edges
Full mid-pass events run 420–511 s (~7–9 min), consistent with ISS-altitude geometry. Several satellites show clipped edge passes (20 s for B1/Nairobi at epoch start; 17 s for B2/London) where the propagation interval catches the tail or head of an arc — these are real passes, truncated at the 00:00 or 24:00 UTC boundary. A multi-day run would merge them with adjacent passes.
Orbital phasing drives per-satellite variation; planes are equivalent overall
Plane B is phased 120° in RAAN from Plane A, and Plane C by 240°. Plane B satellites contact London in the 03–11 h UTC block; Plane C contacts in the 11–19 h block; Plane A in the 00–06 h and 19–24 h blocks. Within each plane, TA offsets of 45° between satellites stagger passes ~90 min apart. Fleet-level London contact is therefore quasi-continuous throughout the day.
