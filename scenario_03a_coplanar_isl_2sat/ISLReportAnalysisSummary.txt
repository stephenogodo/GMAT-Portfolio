Key observations:

1. Steady, monotonic decrease — no oscillation. The range drops smoothly and consistently by about 53 km every 90 minutes throughout the entire 24 hours. This is very different from the 6-satellite case where pairs both stretched and compressed. With only 2 satellites, J2 perturbation is causing them to converge — the 45° gap is slowly closing.

2. Why converging, not oscillating? In the 6-sat case the pairs “trade” perturbation effects — one stretches as its neighbour compresses. With just 2 satellites there’s no such balance. Both are drifting, but LEOSat2 (starting 45° ahead) is drifting slightly faster in the inertial frame, causing the gap to shrink. This is orbital mechanics operating on a 45° separation being driven to close — operationally this would be a conjunction concern over a longer timeframe.

3. Contrast with the 6-sat result: The 6-sat co-planar run showed ranges oscillating between ~5,148 km and ~7,882 km, sometimes exceeding the 7,500 km ISL threshold. The 2-sat run stays well within 5,200 km throughout — never a link-break risk, but a growing proximity risk instead.

4. The 5,187 km starting range is exactly right for 45° separation. Using the chord formula: 2 × 6,778 × sin(22.5°) = 5,183 km — matches perfectly. ✓
